\emph{Weakly supported in its literal form, and not a contribution of learning.} At matched FPR ($\le0.05$ on validation, selected reward $C_{FP}{=}\rlSelCFP$, $c_{obs}{=}\rlSelCObs$) the PPO investigator decides after the first window, with test TPR \rlPpotesttpr{}, FPR \rlPpotestfpr{}, delay \rlPpotestdelay{} steps and observation cost \rlPpotestcost{}, against \rlThrtesttpr{}/\rlThrtestfpr{}/\rlThrtestdelay{}/\rlThrtestcost{} for the fixed threshold and \rlSprttesttpr{}/\rlSprttestfpr{}/\rlSprttestdelay{}/\rlSprttestcost{} for SPRT (Table~\ref{tab:rl}); so delay and cost are lower, as H4 literally requires, but recall is lower than the fixed threshold. The three PPO seeds converged to the same deterministic policy (sd $=0$), and it coincides on every split with a hand-written ``flag iff the first window has a significant edge'' rule that we added post hoc (test TPR \rlFwtesttpr{}, FPR \rlFwtestfpr{}); that rule loses \rlFwDiff{} TPR [\rlFwDiffLo, \rlFwDiffHi] to the fixed threshold. A high false-positive cost makes PPO never alert (\rlNeverAlertCfgs{} of 9 reward settings yield TPR 0). Delay is measured among true positives and the fixed threshold always sees all three windows, so PPO's lower delay and cost follow mechanically from deciding after one window; selection of the reward setting and the comparator thresholds used validation episodes from seen mechanisms only (a first selection on all validation positives chose the same settings and gave identical test numbers); the held-out mechanisms enter only the test and OOD evaluation. Following our decision rule, we report no evidence that RL adds value beyond simple rules in this setting; with three windows per episode there is little sequential structure to learn.
